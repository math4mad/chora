The one idea

One object, studied from five benches. Every function the programme cares about is a sum in an orthogonal basis — and there are exactly three knobs:

Δ = Σn cn φn ,   cn ~ 𝒩(0, λn) ,   φn ⊥ under w(α,β)

Knob I

N — size

How much you are allowed to change: basis terms, rank, parameters.

agreed: dominates

Knob II

λₙ — order

Where in the spectrum the energy sits (σ-position of directions).

killed twice · honestly

Knob III

(α, β) — shape

Where the container allows change: boundary weight, the measure of orthogonality.

open — ours to close

The spectrum-order hypothesis died by pre-registered experiment — once on real LLM weight matrices (MEF), once on a small controlled net (Sarcos). The negative results are kept as load-bearing walls, not embarrassments. What remains — shape — is the contribution.

The Meeting Room

Correspondence is dyadic — letters, immutable, hash-anchored. When one open question puts five verdicts in tension, the benches take their seats. A seat is a context contract: it speaks only from its own bench’s files, signs every turn bench@sha, and resolutions bind the workspace — never a bench’s plan.

The law of the workspace

  1. Share inputs and artifacts, never histories. Four repos, one store; no merges across benches.
  2. Nothing crosses benches without a hash. Every shared byte carries a manifest entry: path, sha256, producer repo@sha.
  3. Single writer, many readers. Produce → hash → append manifest → announce by letter.
  4. Pre-register; keep regimes in separate rows; report retained energy. Never tune on test. One hypothesis check per number.
  5. Negative results are first-class citizens. Never quietly dropped — they are why the third knob is still standing.
